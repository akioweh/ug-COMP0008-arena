#### Question 2 — Floating-Point Representations: bfloat16 vs IEEE 754 Half-Precision

In the training of deep neural networks, practitioners often use floating point representations that are shorter than single-precision. To optimize the accuracy, an alternative to IEEE 754 half-precision (which uses 16 bits separated into a sign bit, 5 exponent bits, and 10 fraction bits) is called `bfloat16`. `bfloat16`, which uses a similar width but with 8 exponent bits and 7 fraction bits, is tailor-suited for such purposes. The exponent biases of IEEE 754 half-precision and `bfloat16` are 15 and 127, respectively.

**Q2a.** Compared with IEEE 754 half-precision, `bfloat16` is: [(+5,+1.5) marks]

- A) Able to represent fewer normal numbers.
- B) Able to represent more integer values accurately.
- C) Able to represent more numbers in the range [1, 2].
- D) Able to represent more numbers in the range [80000, 90000].

**Q2b.** Consider representing $\pi$ in both formats. [(+5,+1.5) marks]

- A) In `bfloat16`, it will have an accurate representation with no error.
- B) In IEEE 754 half-precision it will have an accurate representation with no error.
- C) Both formats will have a representation error, but the error of `bfloat16` will be **lower**.
- D) Both formats will have a representation error, but the error of `bfloat16` will be **higher**.
